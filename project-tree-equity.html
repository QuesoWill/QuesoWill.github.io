Japanese vs. American Leadership Styles of Youth Baseball Coaching

A Quantitative Analysis of Player Performance Outcomes at the Professional Level

The question

Youth baseball coaching in Japan adheres to a strictly Autocratic Leadership style of coaching that is intense and physically demanding, often involving discipline and punishment. American youth baseball coaching tends to follow a more Participative and Democratic Leadership style, with coaches showing more care and encouragement for their players. To determine if coaching styles at the youth level have a measurable impact on performance outcomes at the professional level, a quantitative analysis of baseball statistics is conducted comparing the pitching, hitting and defensive metrics of professional American and Japanese baseball players. With the goal to improve our understanding of effective coaching, by testing the hypothesis that the Japanese coaching style results in more disciplined professional players. Findings are drawn from data comparing statistics of US born players in the MLB and players in NPB, and data comparing Japanese born players playing in the MLB to US born players in the MLB. Which ultimately fails to support the hypothesis, but supports that America is still the best country at baseball.

What I found

For drawing conclusions there are a few key things to note. Firstly, the data collected does not tell a full story. The data collected is not the end all and be all of supporting the hypotheses, other impactful data exists, but was unable to be collected. Secondly, a failure to support a hypothesis does not disprove it, it is just unable to prove it. The previously mentioned advantages of the US have played a role and could be responsible for US favored results, regardless of the impact of coaching style. Lastly, import players are expected to perform better than the average US player. Only star players are imported, it is expensive to import a player (import tax, extra commodities such as translator), so only stars are worth it. Therefore, the import players should perform above the US standard and is only noteworthy when they do not. In terms of ability the hypothesis that American players perform better was supported. For pitchers, Crotin’s research supported that American fastball velocity was higher. Import hitters performed worse after moving to MLB. For hitters, import players hit for less power (lower SLG), and import pitchers performed worse when facing MLB hitters. For defenders, MLB players turned more double plays then NPB players, import fielders had below MLB average OAA, arm strength and an over below average Baseball Savant fielding value. The hypothesis that Japanese players performed better in discipline can not be supported. For pitchers, NPB pitchers hit more batters, threw more wild pitches, and balked more than their US born MLB peers. Import pitchers let up more hits and threw more wild pitches than their US born counterparts. For hitters, there is no data that makes a compelling argument either way. For defenders, NPB catchers allowed for more passed balls. NPB defenders did have a higher fld% then their US born MLB counterparts but by less than a 10th of a percent. Even so, the import players allowed more errors with a fld% that is ~.2% lower. Import defenders did hold an over average arm value, but only by ~2 percentiles which is insignificant based upon the sample size of six qualified defenders. There is no clear evidence that points that either coaching style is more effective than the other.
